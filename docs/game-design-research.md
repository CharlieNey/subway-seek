# Game-design research for Stand Clear

*2026-10-08. Read-only research; nothing in the repo was changed. Sources were read by me and by four parallel research agents; every finding was checked against `DESIGN.md`, `docs/match-balance.md`, `docs/street-view-spike.md`, `docs/DEPLOY.md` and `packages/engine/src/seekerBot.ts` so no implication contradicts a built rule without saying so. Reddit and BoardGameGeek were blocked (403), so the show's subreddit and BGG review threads are absent. **[unverified]** marks a claim taken from a search snippet or a secondary page, not the primary source.*

**Three corrections to what the agents reported, made from the repo:** the spike already measured 20 × `setPano` on one viewer as **0 billable loads** (`docs/street-view-spike.md`, phase C), so Street View cost is not an open question; live rounds **already survive a deploy** (`docs/DEPLOY.md`: sessions saved to SQLite on every change, restored at boot within the 3-minute grace); and a **resume token already exists** (session token in `localStorage`, decision log 2026-10-06). Those findings are kept below only where they still add something.

## 1. Daily puzzle games as a genre

**1.1 The genre's time budget is about three minutes, and its designers defend it.** Wardle: Wordle "encourages you to spend three minutes a day. And that's it." ([UO News](https://news.uoregon.edu/content/wordle-brainchild-uo-alum-sweeping-globe)). Zach Gage on Pile-Up Poker's five hands: "I would like it to be three… you could burn out on" it ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/post/2024/08/interview-game-developer-zach-gage-on-pile-up-poker-and-resisting-dark-patterns/)). Semantle, the harder and longer daily, fell from "a quarter of a million unique IPs every day"; its creator: "I totally understand why people don't want to fry their brains every single day before work" ([hey.gg](https://www.hey.gg/blog/semantle)). No example of a 12-minute daily that retained was found.
*Implication:* a 10–14 real-minute daily is 3–4× the genre and Semantle is the cautionary case. The lever that touches no rule is honesty on the door: "Play today's daily · about 12 minutes" beside the one door on the homepage, so the commitment is chosen rather than discovered. The lever that does touch a rule (an Easy-band daily, ~8 minutes) would undo the 2026-10-06 "daily plays Normal" decision; it should wait for first-round data (see 5.3).

**1.2 "One a day" is friction, not hook, for a cold arrival.** In Travle's Show HN, three of the top comments object to the formula ("no reason to bother", "I'll have forgotten it ever existed in an hour"), and the creator says he wants "unlimited travle, and/or allowing players to catch up". Travle also stores all history client-side in a Huffman-encoded "transfer code" ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38456692)).
*Implication:* the result card already offers "Practice round"; make it the second door on the homepage's "Today's done" state too, not only a countdown. And copy the transfer code: a pasteable string (or `?s=` URL) carrying the tile wall and streak, so a browser wipe isn't a wipe while there is no server record.

**1.3 Clones died when novelty was the only content; the survivors have an editor.** Wordle search interest fell 67% Feb–Sep 2022 ([WordFinder](https://wordfinder.yourdictionary.com/blog/is-wordle-dying-the-data-weighs-in/)); Heardle was shut by Spotify ten months after purchase ([9to5mac](https://9to5mac.com/2023/04/14/heardle-spotify-shutting-down/)). Wyna Liu spends "two to two and a half hours" on each Connections board ([Slate](https://slate.com/life/2024/07/connections-nyt-today-wordle-wyna-liu.html)); Gage: "If a game is fun for 10 hours, it'll be good enough for Puzzmo. Because that's a lot of hours stretched out once a day over a year" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/puzzmo-co-creator-zach-gage-on-building-newspaper-games-that-can-last-forever)).
*Implication:* the daily is a date-seeded draw, which is the clone shape. The cheapest editorial layer is one hand-written line a day on the **"yesterday's hider" tablet** ("Twins: 7 Av and Prospect Park answer every question from Atlantic the same way; the Q line question split them"). Ten minutes a day, and it is the content a share link lands on.

**1.4 The share grid works because it tells a story, not a score.** Wardle took the grid from a player in New Zealand ([tweet](https://x.com/powerlanguish/status/1471493886031773707)); what reads at a glance is shape: how close, how fast, whether it failed.
*Implication:* "📡🗽🐙" says which questions, not what happened. Note that the game already shows the count after each answer ("59 stations → 6" on the express band); only the pre-ask preview is withheld. So a post-hoc story line is within the rules: a second line like "97 → 25 → 7 → 3 · wrong station once · seen at 40 m", and a miss as a struck-through tile. Needs a decision-log row because the share is a decided four-line form.

**1.5 Streak loss is the loudest complaint in the genre; the fix is a held, silent freeze.** 5.6 million Wordle streaks ended on one day in 2024 ([AOL/exclusive](https://www.aol.com/exclusive-wordle-puzzle-ended-5-013624721.html)); NYT now requires an account to keep stats ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/puzzle/wordle-stats-new-york-times-account-required/)). GeoGuessr shipped a daily streak freeze in 2026 after a request with a 796-day player on it ([Canny](https://geoguessr.canny.io/feature-requests/p/daily-challenge-streak-freeze)) and widened the streak so Duels count too ([patch notes](https://geoguessr.canny.io/changelog/patch-notes-august-21st-2026)). Duolingo's freeze is "already in the user's pocket… no popup" ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/streaks)).
*Implication:* un-park "streak repair once a month" as a held freeze granted automatically and applied silently, drawn on the tile wall as a blank tile, not a struck one. And since a 12-minute daily will be skipped more than a 3-minute one, let a practice or You-hide round keep the streak on a skipped day, as GeoGuessr did.

**1.6 Archives arrived officially, with "resume" and separate stats.** NYT opened the Wordle archive (1,000+ puzzles) in May 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/05/07/nyt-games-wordle-archive-access-1000-past-puzzles)) and a Connections archive with "resume unfinished games" ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/nyt-subscribers-now-play-old-153332822.html)); the third-party Wordle Archive drew 100,000 visitors a day before the takedown ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/wordle-archive-shuts-down-after-new-york-times-issues-legal-demand/1100-6501588/)).
*Implication:* the seed makes an archive free, but the homepage's "yesterday's hider" spoils yesterday, so a playable archive starts the day before. Archive plays fill tiles in a different ink and never touch the streak or "fastest find". The open question "keep a solo round until midnight" (DESIGN, Open questions) is the Connections "resume" point and matters more for a 12-minute round than for a 3-minute one.

## 2. Asymmetric hidden-information and deduction games

**2.1 Fixed reveal timetables are what make the hider's side a game rather than a wait.** Scotland Yard's Mr X reveals on rounds 3, 8, 13, 18, 24, and the detectives always see the ticket type ([Nijssen & Winands, IEEE TCIAIG 2012](https://dke.maastrichtuniversity.nl/m.winands/documents/TCAIG_ScotlandYard.pdf)). A strategy note: "never use a bus or train ticket unless doing so does not reveal extra information about your location" ([incoherency.co.uk](https://incoherency.co.uk/blog/stories/scotland-yard.html)). The hider plans *around* the reveal.
*Implication:* the Phase 1 status board (Riding / Changing trains / Walking) is already the ticket-type leak; the parked route-letter reveal is the surface turn. If it's ever adopted, make it one fixed, pre-announced moment (10:10, shown on both screens) so the hider can time a transfer before it, as Mr X times a double move. A continuous route-letter feed would remove that decision.

**2.2 The hider's fun is overhearing the hunt, not only seeing it.** Shut Up & Sit Down on Whitehall Mystery: "how delicious it is to listen to all of your friends talk about you when you're right there"; "someone routinely outlining your exact plan… only for the conversation to move on" ([SU&SD](https://www.shutupandsitdown.com/review-whitehall-mystery/)). Space-Biff on Specter Ops: "watching your pursuers stumble around, absolutely certain you're in one place" ([Space-Biff](https://spacebiff.com/2015/04/24/specter-ops/)).
*Implication:* "the hider watches the seeker's live dot" covers the seeing. The overhearing equivalent is the seeker's **armed but unconfirmed chip** in select-see-confirm: a status line on the hider's screen, "Seeker is weighing a Radar question", without the shape. In solo the bot could arm 3 real seconds before it asks (its action floor). In M3 it gives the hider the Whitehall moment of hearing the right idea get dropped; arming a decoy is free, which is a mind game rather than a leak, since the hider already sees everything else.

**2.3 Timing is the leak every table game fights, and digital versions fix it with fixed clocks.** "A lot of the game of Scotland Yard is played not on the board but in your physical presence around the table" ([Meeple Like Us](https://www.meeplelikeus.co.uk/scotland-yard-1983/)). The Nintendo DS AI "often plays immediately, but it sometimes takes 5–10 seconds to find a move" (Nijssen & Winands, above), which is a tell.
*Implication:* the hider's answer window already fixes the card tell. Two channels remain: (a) a Veto or Randomize is "visible when it lands" and must land on the exact tick an answer would (the engine does this; keep a test on it); (b) the bot seeker's 3-real-second action floor must not scale with its candidate count or search depth, or a human hider reads "the bot is thinking" as "few stations left". Use a seeded fixed delay, never a measured one.

**2.4 The deduction cliff: "found" must not mean "fizzle".** Meeple Like Us on Scotland Yard's end: "When the Real Police know the location of a suspect, they don't hail a taxi"; the game can "fizzle out rather than reach an exciting catharsis". Fury of Dracula's answer is combat ([Polyhedron Collider](https://www.polyhedroncollider.com/2016/08/fury-of-dracula-review.html)); Letters from Whitechapel's is escalating constraint: Jack's coach and alley tokens shrink each night so "the last night is mostly a foot slog" ([Everything Board Games](https://everythingboardgames.com/2025/04/everything-board-games-letters-from-whitechapel-review-from-carl-king.html)).
*Implication:* the street search is Stand Clear's combat and at 38% of seeking (Normal, `match-balance.md`) it's the right size. The fizzle is the wrong-station visit (0.62 a round on Normal): ~4 game-min learning nothing. One agent proposed telling the seeker "Right station" when the hider gets "Seeker is in your zone"; **that contradicts the decided rule** that the seeker gets no zone confirmation (decision log 2026-10-05, "M4 solo: finding the station starts the endgame but isn't revealed"), so it's listed here as a challenge to a logged decision, not a recommendation. The Whitechapel-shaped alternative that keeps the rule: a hider's held time bonuses could decay once the seeker is in the zone, so the endgame tightens on the hider's side too. Needs `pnpm sim:match`.

**2.5 Adversarial answering is honest but reads as mocking unless the rule is known.** Absurdle "picks the word coloring which prunes the possible word list the least" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absurdle)); players describe it as "deliberately mocking your efforts" **[unverified: snippet]**.
*Implication:* the bot hider's kill-shot Veto ("a question expected to leave ≤10 stations") is least-useful-truth and is fair because it reads only public information, but the seeker only feels the fairness if told the rule. Print it on the closed chip's caption: "Vetoed · the hider vetoes any question that would leave ten stations or fewer". Also keep the share trail type-only (as decided): question *order* plus a visible veto now reconstructs the policy.

**2.6 Digital Scotland Yards were judged on the AI's visible pointless moves, and hider AI is the harder role.** Pocket Gamer: "dim-witted AI players that frequently skew the challenge from one extreme to another" ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/scotland-yard/review/)). Nijssen & Winands' MCTS beat the DS program 34–16, winning 11 as hider to the DS's 2. Fury of Dracula digital's complaints are legibility: Dracula will "go in the sea with no reason at all" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/958530/discussions/0/2979655949472847907/)).
*Implication:* the bot **seeker**, not the bot hider, is where Stand Clear will be judged, because the human hider watches every move. The 303-real-second quiet ride is the "go in the sea" moment. See 3.1 and 7.2.

## 3. Pacing in real-time-ish single-player

**3.1 Tolerance for an opponent's "thinking" is seconds, not minutes.** A turn-based-game thread puts it at "10–15 seconds tops" with something watchable and "5 seconds tops" with only an icon ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/forums/topic/467197-acceptable-wait-times-for-a-turn-based-game/4074341/), **[unverified: 403; from snippet]**). Fabian Fischer: opponents "should ideally be acting instantly" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/a-different-take-on-turn-timers)).
*Implication:* the 3-second action floor is fine; the 303-second ride is 20× over the watchable ceiling. `seekerBot.ts` shows why: on a train the bot asks only when a category is ready **and** a question from its origin is worth ≥0.25 bits; after four questions every category is cooling (15/15/15/10/40 min) and it rides to the candidates. A question every gap would cap quiet time at ~50 real seconds but raise the hider's bonus, so it's a balance change (`pnpm sim:match`). The non-balance fix is making the ride a countdown rather than a void: the hider's status board shows the next public event ("Seeker's train reaches 59 St in 40 s" and "Next question possible at 10:52"), derived from the public timetable and cooldowns the hider already sees.

**3.2 Animation stacked on a fixed cost is what players hate.** Terraforming Mars digital: "They need to speed animation at least 8X, + confirmations is such a pain" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/800270/discussions/0/1733216261951899848/)). Dire Wolf's Root added "Skip Replay" in patch 1.31.3 "to get you back into the action faster" ([Dire Wolf](https://news.direwolfdigital.com/root-patch-1-31-3-fast-forward-to-fun/)).
*Implication:* the hider's answer window (10 real s) is the game's one deliberate fixed cost; nothing may stack on it. The ten-tile timer should start the instant Ask is confirmed and the answer should land with no reveal animation (the motion catalog's 300 ms ceiling already says so; keep it true under the band's "collapse into the log" too). The replay already scrubs; keep a one-key "to the end".

**3.3 Pause-on-event is loved and resented in the same breath.** CK3 pauses on every event and can't be told not to; CK2 let players set it per message category; at top speed players "can't react in time" ([Paradox forum](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/threads/is-autopause-on-events-a-setting.1445451/), **[unverified: behind browser check; from snippet]**).
*Implication:* DESIGN already names "event-by-event skipping, stopping before a human answer or keep decision" as the fix for the hider's dead time, and the motion rule says fast-forward makes the clock later, never faster. The CK2 lesson is to pick the stop set carefully, as the **hider's own decisions and the seeker's public state changes**: a question landing, a keep choice, the seeker boarding or alighting, surfacing, entering the zone. Not every station the seeker's train calls at. Skip is unavailable while a decision is pending, which answers the stated reason for the S3 "no seeking skip" exception.

**3.4 Football Manager's answer to dead time is auto-continue when nothing is pending.** FM's continue timeout "continue[s] the game automatically without the player having to press continue"; one player says a 2-second timeout "at least halved the amount of time it takes me to complete a season" ([the9llamas](https://www.the9llamas.com/technology/speeding-up-football-manager)); FM26 added Instant Result ([Operation Sports](https://www.operationsports.com/football-manager-26-adds-instant-result-option-for-matches/)).
*Implication:* an *auto* skip (the clock jumps on its own when the hider has nothing pending) would break rule 1 (the clock is later, not faster) only if drawn as speed; drawn as the existing fast-forward step, it is a one-toggle variant of 3.3 for practice You-hide rounds, and never for M3, where the clock is shared. Worth a line in DESIGN as a proposal, not a rule.

**3.5 Resume speed after a stop is a real design question.** Dinosaur Polo Club on Mini Metro: "We're still not sure on resuming the game at 1x speed, instead of what it was paused from"; players wanted the prior speed kept ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287980/discussions/0/611696927915807886/)).
*Implication:* when a skip stops at an event, the clock is at 1× by construction (6× shared). Keep the ▸▸ button lit as a one-tap "skip again to the next event", so the hider never re-chooses.

**3.6 GeoGuessr Duels bound idle by the opponent's action, not the clock.** Both start at 6,000 health; "as soon as the first player makes a guess, the countdown clock will begin and the other player will be required to make their guess within 15 seconds"; multipliers climb in 0.5 steps; the healing round was removed as low-impact ([Plonk It](https://www.plonkit.net/beginners-guide); [GeoGuessr Guide](https://geoguessrguide.com/blog-geoguessr-ranked-guide.html); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGuessr_World_Championship)).
*Implication:* the hider's idle between questions is already bounded by the 5-minute gap (50 real s). Make it as legible as Duels' 15-second bar: a five-tile "Next question possible" timer on the hider's status board, the mirror of the seeker's gap timer on the Ask tab. For M3 the first-mover rule is worth a proposal: the player who surfaces first in round 1 sets the round-2 hide length. Not a rule yet.

## 4. Street View as a game surface

**4.1 Street View's price shaped GeoGuessr's whole business; Stand Clear has already measured its own.** GeoGuessr removed its free tier around 1 Feb 2024, keeping only the Daily Challenge free; "every single Street View panorama GeoGuessr loads runs through Google's Street View API" ([EraGuessr](https://eraguessr.ai/guides/why-geoguessr-is-not-free); [WhereAmI](https://whereamigame.app/blog/is-geoguessr-free-in-2026/)). Current list: Dynamic Street View $14/1,000 after 5,000 free a month, Static $7/1,000 after 10,000; the $200 credit ended March 2025 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/billing-and-pricing/pricing)).
*Implication:* the spike's phase C already showed 20 × `setPano` on one viewer bills nothing, so the endgame's map-driven walk costs 1–3 loads a round. The Photo question is the one per-event cost (~$0.007 a Static image). The daily quotas, not the $10 budget alert, are what stop a bad day (8.6). Nothing to change in rules; keep "one viewer, `setPano`" as an engineering rule in DESIGN so a refactor can't lose it.

**4.2 The free clones ride the Embed API, which can't run Stand Clear's endgame and is wrong for its photos.** WorldGuessr "uses the Google Maps Streetview Embed API, which is completely free" ([Maps Mania](https://googlemapsmania.blogspot.com/2024/08/worldguessr-on-street-view.html)); the Embed SKU is unlimited and free ([StoreRocket](https://storerocket.io/learn/google-maps-api-pricing)), but exposes no JS events.
*Implication:* one agent suggested the Photo question as an embed instead of a Static URL. Reject it: an embed carries Google's navigation arrows, so the seeker could **walk around the hider's spot** from the photo. The Static image is the NMPZ photo the rule wants. The 40 m reveal and "everywhere walked counts as searched" need the Dynamic API's position events, which the embed lacks.

**4.3 NMPZ shows a single static look is a complete game.** NMPZ is "one of the most popular competitive formats… because it rewards deep pattern recognition over patient exploration" ([GeoGuessr Guide](https://geoguessrguide.com/blog-geoguessr-nmpz-tips.html)); World Cup matches alternate Move / No Move / NMPZ ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GeoGuessr_World_Championship)).
*Implication:* the directional Photo (N/E/S/W/up) is a strength, not a cheap substitute. On the hider's **Finding a spot** screen, preview the four Static shots the seeker could buy from where you stand, so both players judge the spot on identical evidence; today the hider sees the live pano, not the seeker's cropped frame. Four Static loads per preview is ~$0.03; cap it to the spot being considered, not every pano walked.

**4.4 Moving pano-to-pano has been the weak point since 2013, and Google's fixes are presentational.** Jay Is Games on Pursued (2013): "The biggest flaw of Pursued is the rough periods of long loading times that occur as you move from location to location" ([JIG](https://jayisgames.com/review/pursued.php)). Google's 2016 renderer added frames so transitions "glid[e] smoothly" ([Google Maps Platform](https://mapsplatform.google.com/resources/blog/new-javascript-street-view-renderer/)); Daydream Street View used "a fade or dissolve transition when teleporting" ([Google](https://blog.google/products/google-ar-vr/daydream-labs-locomotion-vr/)); VR research warns that "continuous and animated rotations tend to induce motion sickness" ([arXiv 2410.20675](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.20675)).
*Implication:* map-driven walking already sidesteps the slideshow because the dot's linear walk is the state and the pano is presentation (rule 1 and the engineering principle "motion is presentation"). Two refinements: prefetch the arrival pano while the walking band fills, dissolve on arrival; and **snap** the heading to the walk direction rather than animating the turn. The direction tiles should keep moving the dot on the map, never step pano by pano.

**4.5 Worldboard's endgame is Stand Clear's endgame, minus the look and the pace.** Worldboard: games "typically 1–3 hours", save and resume, free with ads, membership removes ads and allows "80×" clock speed; "Once you're on foot inside the hiding zone, the hider freezes where they stand. Get within 25 m of them on foot to win"; Street View appears as a question type ([Worldboard](https://worldboard.games/hide-and-seek)).
*Implication:* the freeze and the 25 m catch are not differentiators; the 40 m reveal, the 8 m searched grid, the circle around the seeker's *own* station, and walking inside Street View are. Resist a paid speed toggle: Worldboard sells speed because its rounds are too long; Stand Clear's fixed 6× with event-stopping skip is the craft claim. One test to run: whether clicking a gold square in a pano at ≤25 m feels like finding someone or like a map click with a photo behind it.

**4.6 "Find a person in Street View" has never shipped; every attempt collapses to guess-the-place.** hideandseek.world: "Other players will use Street View to figure out where you're hiding… The closer your guess, the more points" ([hideandseek.world](https://hideandseek.world/)); Pursued, Geotastic and City Guesser are all place-guessing ([Maps Mania](https://googlemapsmania.blogspot.com/2017/09/10-street-view-games-to-kill-your-day.html)). The DC Metro home game's endgame was seekers who "amble around" a strip mall for 13 minutes before a photo ended it ([mweepigeon](https://mweepigeon.gay/2025/06/28/hide-seek-dc/)).
*Implication:* no prior art teaches that a hider is a marker that appears at 40 m. The three-line "What counts" notice carries the whole genre's onboarding. Make the first 40 m reveal of a player's first round a conductor's-voice moment ("There. Forty metres.") so the rule is learned by event; and don't try to draw the hider into the imagery, which Google's terms make moot anyway.

## 5. Onboarding and first-round design

**5.1 George Fan's PvZ rules go beyond eight words: "teach one new thing per level" and "the first zombie doesn't come until you have planted."** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-how-i-got-my-mom-to-play-through-i-plants-vs-zombies-i-)).
*Implication:* the three hints obey the word limit; what they skip is the one-thing-per-level cut. The Ask tab shows seven families to a first-time seeker at 10:20. Proposal: on a browser's first round only, the Ask tab shows Radar and Matching (the two most legible shapes under select-see-confirm), and Thermometer, Tentacles and Photos appear from round two. The station and the deck stay shared, so `?d=23&t=41` still compares.

**5.2 Into the Breach: rules that exist only as consequences are never learned.** Justin Ma: "sacrifice cool ideas for the sake of clarity every time"; animated tooltips were "a thousand times more effective" than text ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-into-the-breach-i-dev-on-ui-design-sacrifice-cool-ideas-for-the-sake-of-clarity-every-time-)). Players missed Reset Turn entirely: "I had no idea until I happened across a mention… in an article" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/590380/discussions/0/1694914736008198978/)).
*Implication:* "Veto closes the question" is a consequence rule. The veto line already says "That question is gone for the round"; make it name the question ("Radar ½ mi is closed this round") and strike the chip at the same moment, so the rule is learned when it bites. Same for "Normal holds movement curses until ≤20 stations": one line when the first curse lands.

**5.3 Baba Is You: the designer's intuition about difficulty was wrong.** Teikari: "even things that I felt personally were too easy or too obvious mostly turned out to be very very difficult" ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/)); the "you are playing the tutorial" answer was accepted by the asker ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/736260/discussions/0/2666626567708461388)).
*Implication:* "Normal = 5–6 questions" is a simulated-strong-seeker number with no human data. Don't argue Easy-vs-Normal for the first daily; add a `first_round` flag to the JSONL round logs (set from the browser's empty history at round start) and read questions-to-pin, wrong stations and found-rate for first rounds separately. Switch only on evidence.

**5.4 Don't make the first daily Easy; make it Normal-station, Easy-play.** Marvel Snap pits new players "exclusively against AI-controlled opponents" until rank 10 without saying so ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/marvel-snap-bots-rank-cheating-odin-hulk-easy-wins-1849710078)); Hearthstone's tutorial is scripted so "the player cannot lose", then new players meet only new players ([wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Tutorial); [Brode](https://www.hearthpwn.com/news/2145-ben-brode-on-the-basic-decks-new-player-experience)); Slay the Spire's Neow's Lament is offered only after a failed run ([wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Neow's_Lament)).
*Implication:* an Easy-band first daily changes the hidden station and breaks "same station and deck for everyone". Instead borrow the Easy band's *play* for a browser's first daily: the bot plays no curses (Fog and Signal loss hide exactly the screens the hints teach) and no kill-shot veto. Neow-style, a missed first daily defaults the next practice round's picker to Easy. Caveat: the friend's `t` is then not like-for-like; the share could carry a flag, or this applies to practice only.

**5.5 Scripted tutorials bleed players; teaching in place doesn't.** A Roblox dev measured "41% of players leaving game within 30 seconds of tutorial" and 30% finishing a five-minute one ([DevForum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/41-of-players-leaving-game-within-30-seconds-of-tutorial/3814365)); Wordle: "this should only ask for three minutes of your time" ([TIME](https://time.com/6143715/wordle-sale-josh-wardle-interview/)).
*Implication:* a shorter scripted first round is a five-minute tutorial by another name; skip it. The first-round dead zone is structural: a first seeker waits a 10-real-second answer window on question one, then 50 real seconds of gap before question two. Confirm no gap applies before the first question (true today), fire hint 1 at 10:20 exactly rather than on the first hover, and consider letting the hint 2 moment ("Ride toward what's left") coincide with the departure board's first boardable row lighting up.

**5.6 Portal: fix the environment, not the text.** A playtester "spent half an hour trying to push a shelf onto a button, while completely ignoring a nearby box" ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon)); Valve tested weekly from week one.
*Implication:* "What counts" is the text fix. The environment fix is that the 400 m circle and the shaded 8 m cells are on the strip before the first walk tile is enabled, so the first move can't be the shelf (walking out of the circle). And a Portal-sized test, three friends, first five real minutes only, watching whether they find the Ask tab without hint 1, is cheaper than any M3 work and the one thing no simulation gives.

## 6. Retention and virality without accounts or ads

**6.1 The share link is already a challenge link; GeoGuessr shows the no-account form.** GeoGuessr challenge links need no account, only a nickname, and compare scores on a shared board ([Playbite](https://www.playbite.com/q/how-to-challenge-friend-of-geoguessr), secondary); Wardle: "You can just give someone the link, and they can play" ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/wardle-on-wordle-simple-word-game-global-phenomenon/)).
*Implication:* `?d=23&t=41` already carries the friend's time. Keep `t` through the round and print it on the result card: "Friend 41 min · You 52 min". A two-person board with no server. An optional `n=` nickname makes the arriving band read "Sam found Daily #23 in 41 min."

**6.2 Ranks beat boards: Spelling Bee's Genius is relative to the day, not to other players.** Genius is 70% of the day's points; "about 25 percent of players will achieve Genius at least once" a week ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/reading/the-genius-of-the-new-york-times-spelling-bee/)); the Bee's community formed around hints, not a leaderboard ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times_Spelling_Bee)).
*Implication:* un-park Express / Local / Shuttle and define them against the **day's par**, which the deterministic bot gives for free: the hardness band's expected questions and the sim's median find (38 game-min Normal). Express = under par; the Queen Bee equivalent = no wrong station and ≤3 questions. Needs no server. Watch the collision with the card names (Delays, Planned work).

**6.3 Global leaderboards don't measurably retain; local comparison does.** Quantum Moves found no strong retention difference between no board, top-5 and relative boards ([arXiv 1707.03704](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.03704)); JMIR 2021: macro boards accumulate "failure experiences" for lower-ranked users, micro boards give "more success opportunities" ([JMIR Serious Games](https://games.jmir.org/2021/2/e14746)); Duolingo's Friend Streak makes users "22% more likely to complete their daily lesson" ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://duolingo.deconstructoroffun.com/mechanics/streaks)).
*Implication:* skip the global board; it needs server-authoritative results, and the decision log already notes the give-up-and-replay exploit "only fools the cheater's own browser" today. "Fastest find" as the PR plus the share-link compare is the micro board.

**6.4 Async 1v1 without accounts: Lichess's player-secret URL.** Lichess lets an anonymous player join by link; the public game id is 8 characters and the player's private link adds 4 ([Lichess forum](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/what-is-a-lichess-game-id); [anonymous invites](https://lichess.org/forum/lichess-feedback/how-to-invite-an-unregistered-friend-to-play)). Chess.com requires both to have accounts ([Chess.com forum](https://www.chess.com/forum/view/help-support/how-to-invite-friend-who-does-not-have-chess-com-account-to-play)).
*Implication:* for M3 a match is `/m/<8-char id>/<4-char player secret>`, secret in `localStorage`, match state in SQLite (the session table already has the shape, per DEPLOY). This is the first thing that genuinely needs a server record. See idea 2 for the async bridge it enables.

**6.5 "The result page is the homepage."** Wordle's post-solve state (stats, streak, countdown, share) is the only screen most players see after day one; Semantle puts yesterday's word on it ([Semantle FAQ](https://semantle.com/faq/)); the Connections archive adds resume and a per-day bot analysis ([Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/entertainment/nyt-subscribers-now-play-old-153332822.html)).
*Implication:* the homepage's "Today's done · found in 52 min" plate is the right instinct; make it carry the full result (score, trail, the replay scrubber inline, share, the friend compare from 6.1) with the countdown and the tile wall under it. "Yesterday's hider" is the Semantle move; the replay is the WordleBot move.

**6.6 What needs a server record vs `localStorage`.** `localStorage` carries the streak, tile wall, PR, a held freeze, the friend's `t`, the session token. A Travle-style transfer code makes it portable ([HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38456692)). A server record is needed for M3 match halves and player secrets, any unspoofable board, and cross-device streaks; NYT's lesson is that the moment people care about a streak, loss on migration is the top complaint ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/news/wordle-disaster-some-players-lose-their-streaks-in-transfer-to-nyt)).

## 7. Bot opponents players respect

**7.1 Luck gets read as cheating; disclosed bonuses get accepted.** Notes on Soren Johnson's "Playing to Lose": "AI luck often construed as cheating"; cheating "should not feel unfair" ([CivFanatics](https://forums.civfanatics.com/threads/playing-to-lose-ai-and-civilization-soren-johnson.377317/), a listener's summary). Civ manuals "detail exactly how much the computer cheats… at various difficulty levels" ([TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/TheComputerIsACheatingBastard/Civilization)).
*Implication:* the bot hider doesn't cheat, but a Hard "twin" will feel like it did when the seeker's split goes wrong. Disclose the band like a Civ manual on the round-start tablet: "Today's hider: Normal band · chosen from the date before the round · 5–6 questions to pin". The hardness band is Stand Clear's difficulty bonus; name it.

**7.2 Hide the misplay where information is already hidden, never in the open.** Hearthstone's practice AI "will sometimes intentionally choose an option that is not the highest-rated"; because the hand is hidden, "less than optimal choices are usually not apparent to the player" ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Design_and_development_of_Hearthstone)).
*Implication:* the human hider sees every question and the live dot, so a dumb ride is a visible misplay; the bot seeker's *question choice* and its ≤4/3/2 surfacing threshold are where Easy and Normal can be weaker invisibly. The 303-second ride is 30 game-min with every category cooling; if the ride must stay, the bot's weakness should never show as "did nothing" but as "asked the second-best question", which the hider can't tell from the first.

**7.3 Telegraph the rule, then let the outcome be the player's fault.** Justin Ma: "every death felt like your own fault… telegraphed enemy attacks as a core mechanic" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)); Terry Cavanagh on Dicey Dungeons: enemies "always do the same things, but then there's this random element that feels very fair" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/witch-craft-how-i-dicey-dungeons-i-balances-chance-and-predictability)).
*Implication:* "goes up at ≤3 stations" is an intent. The difficulty hint already says "Searches with 3 stations left"; add the moment itself for the hider, mirroring the seeker's hint 3: "Three left. They're coming up." Then a catch is the hider's spot choice, not the bot's cunning.

**7.4 Human-like bots are liked until they repeat; adaptive bots feel toyed with.** Lichess on Maia: "the repetitive openings could become boring"; "always play the same variations" ([Lichess forum](https://lichess.org/forum/general-chess-discussion/maia-chess-a-human-like-neural-network-chess-engine?page=4)). Chess.com's adaptive bots "make stronger moves when losing and weaker moves when winning" and the reviewer felt it ([Chess.com blog](https://www.chess.com/blog/AdviceCabinet/are-the-chess-com-adaptive-bots-truly-adaptive)).
*Implication:* the daily stays deterministic, but in practice the bot seeker's thrifty opening will repeat from a given hub after a few games. Break ties within a small bits-per-minute margin with the round seed. Never add rubber-banding; Hunicke's DDA warning is already in DESIGN, and the S3 bands are fixed before the round, which is right.

**7.5 Make the agenda public, keep the secret secret, explain each move once afterwards.** Ed Beach on Civ VI: AI "driven by… leader agendas" to fix Civ V's opacity ([Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/gaming/civilization-vi-first-details/)); poker tooling sells the post-hoc "why" ([GTO Wizard](https://blog.gtowizard.com/gto-wizard-ai-explained/)).
*Implication:* the replay log already lists every question with its count. Give each bot decision one line, in the counts that are fine after the round: "Vetoed: on the public map this would have left 8 of 23." "Held Wrong way: 24 left, plays at 20." "Went up: 3 left; looking is cheaper than asking." "Asked Radar 1 mi: best bits per minute, 49 → 25." The bot hider's agenda ("vetoes from the public map only, never from the true answer") goes on the round-start tablet; the station is its hidden agenda.

**7.6 "Input reading" is the accusation to pre-empt.** MK11 players: "It reads you input commands as you press them" ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/976310/discussions/0/1679190184077398354/)).
*Implication:* the public-info Veto already defends the bot hider. The bot seeker's exposed side is the sweep: its cell order is a declared rule (nearest live cell on the public grid) that never reads the spot, and the replay should draw the sweep path, so a quick 40 m reveal reads as method.

## 8. Small multiplayer web games by solo devs

**8.1 One cheap box survives a launch; lag is the risk, and here lag has a rule attached.** generals.io's #1-HN day: "50k users… on a $20 DigitalOcean droplet… There was definitely some lag, but everyone could play!" ([lobste.rs](https://lobste.rs/s/akzlu5/how_build_multiplayer_io_game)); Semantle served a quarter-million IPs a day for "$50 or $60 a month" ([hey.gg](https://www.hey.gg/blog/semantle)).
*Implication:* the EC2 box is not the launch risk. But the hider's answer window is exactly 1 game-min so timing can't leak a card; under event-loop lag it stretches unevenly. Log "answer delivered at +N s vs the 10 s window" in the playtest metrics; a drifting p95 means the leak-proofing rule is silently broken.

**8.2 At small scale what breaks is per-match state, not connections.** A Node/Socket.io game pegged a 1-vCPU droplet at "2 concurrent games (approximately 10 players)" because events weren't deleted; one line fixed it ([freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/an-embarrassing-tale-why-my-server-could-only-handle-10-players-3b83b6fa8136/)).
*Implication:* a round holds the 8 m grid beliefs, the question log and the replay. Add a `sim`-style soak test that runs ~50 rounds in one process and asserts heap doesn't grow, and serve the post-round replay from the SQLite row, not the live round object.

**8.3 Outages ruin in-progress games; Stand Clear already covers this, M3 must reuse it.** Board Game Arena: "since BGA is a realtime service, any outage may ruin games in progress"; their ceiling was one component, the main server ([BGA forum](https://forum.boardgamearena.com/viewtopic.php?f=5&t=14646)).
*Implication:* DEPLOY already saves live sessions to SQLite and restores them within the 3-minute grace. The M3 rule to write now: a two-player room is one session row with two player secrets, so the same restore covers it, and a deploy during a match pauses both clocks (the two-player pause-on-drop is already decided).

**8.4 Tiny populations: don't build a public queue; make the bot the fill.** A party-game devlog: "sometimes you just don't have a party", so bots fill seats and games can be flipped public ([itch.io](https://rolyanm.itch.io/stuck-for-words/devlog/1609816/never-short-of-players-again-bots-public-games)). Worldboard offers solo-vs-AI and 1v1 only, with 1–3 hour games and save/resume ([Worldboard](https://worldboard.games/hide-and-seek)). No data on daily-to-multiplayer conversion was found **[unverified: none exists publicly]**.
*Implication:* "Play a friend · Not in service" should open as invite-link only, no stranger matchmaking. The near-async bridge that needs no simultaneous presence: **"Hide for a friend"**, you ride Phase 1 and lock a spot, the link carries a token, and the bot hider plays your hand with its existing public-info Veto policy against your friend. Every piece exists (bot hider, seeded deck, visibility filter). You lose "watching the net close", so it's a bridge, not M3.

**8.5 Invite friction lands on the invitee.** "The people most likely to quit are the ones you invited, not the host"; five characters is the limit for codes read aloud; use codes for short sessions and accounts for people who return across weeks ([dev.to](https://dev.to/board_it_b11ff7d58bf863f8/why-a-five-character-room-code-beats-accounts-for-a-party-game-21mo)); Jackbox uses 4 letters because they're read off a TV ([Jackbox](https://support.jackboxgames.com/hc/en-us/articles/15794759479959-How-do-I-join-a-game)).
*Implication:* desktop-first and pasted into chat means a URL, not a code; extend `?d=23&t=41`. The invitee's quit point in Stand Clear is Phase 1: an invited seeker staring at a status board for 3.5 real minutes. In an async match the host hides before sending the link, so the invitee's first screen is 10:20 with questions live, as solo rounds already start. The arriving band: "Charlie is hidden. Your turn."

**8.6 A budget alert stops nothing; quotas do; retry loops are the real bill.** A Street View project ran up ~$800 from a failing map ID with no `gm_authFailure` handler and an autopilot firing "every 3 s indefinitely, even if every call is failing" ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/ford442/webgpu_streetview/issues/75)). Google: "Setting a budget does not cap API usage"; per-API daily quotas do ([Maps FAQ](https://developers.google.com/maps/faq)); Street View metadata is its own SKU ([Google](https://developers.google.com/maps/documentation/streetview/usage-and-billing)).
*Implication:* keep the $10 alert, but set daily quotas on Maps JS loads, Dynamic Street View, Static Street View and Static Metadata separately. Photos are bounded by price and cooldown at ~8 per player per round, so the quota is derivable from the logged metrics. Add a `gm_authFailure` handler and make a failed pano load inside the "walk to this corner" loop a one-shot refusal, never a retry.

## Ten ideas worth brainstorming

Ranked by expected impact against cost. Each names the rule or screen it changes and the risk. None is decided; each would need a DESIGN.md proposal and a decision-log row.

**1. Event-by-event skip for the hider in You hide.** The 303-real-second quiet ride is the one measured failure, and DESIGN already names this as the fix. Stop set = the hider's own decisions (question lands, keep choice) plus the seeker's public state changes (boards, alights, surfaces, enters the zone); skip unavailable while any decision is pending, so it can never eat the answer window. *Changes:* the S3 "no seeking-phase skip" exception, the clock tile's ▸▸ in You hide. *Risk:* collapses the bot's watchable pace, which is the point; in M3 the clock is shared, so it stays solo-only.

**2. "Hide for a friend": the async bridge before M3.** You ride Phase 1 and press Hide here; the link carries a token; the bot hider plays your hand with its public-info Veto policy against your friend, who starts at 10:20 as solo rounds do. Needs the first server record (a match row with Lichess-style player secrets). *Changes:* "Play a friend · Not in service" becomes "Hide for a friend · Now boarding"; the share-link pattern. *Risk:* the hider loses watching the net close and the Veto choice; a replayed link must be refused after one play.

**3. Par ranks in the subway's words.** Express / Local / Shuttle against the day's par (hardness band × sim median), no server. Gives a "done for today" short of perfection, as Spelling Bee's Genius does. *Changes:* result card, share line, tile wall tint. *Risk:* tier names collide with card names (Delays, Planned work); par is a bot number until first-round human data exists (idea 10).

**4. One line per bot decision in the replay log.** "Vetoed: would have left 8 of 23." "Went up: 3 left." Counts are shown after answers already, so this breaks no rule; it turns the bot from an oracle into an opponent with a published agenda. *Changes:* Replay log rows, the round-start tablet ("Normal band · vetoes from the public map only"). *Risk:* none to balance; some copy budget.

**5. Consequence rules spoken when they bite.** The veto line names the closed question; the first 40 m reveal of a first round gets a conductor's line ("There. Forty metres."); the first curse states Normal's hold rule. *Changes:* the Voice table (it is "twelve fixed moments"; adding two breaks the number, so either replace or renumber), the closed chip's caption. *Risk:* diluting the voice; the pass's rule is personality at few moments only.

**6. A held streak freeze, and practice keeps the streak.** Granted silently once a month, shown as a blank tile; a practice or You-hide round on a skipped day keeps the streak, as GeoGuessr did in 2026. Plus a Travle-style transfer code for the tile wall. *Changes:* homepage stats, tile wall, `localStorage` schema. *Risk:* a 12-minute daily skipped often makes the streak mean less; the code is one more thing to explain.

**7. The hider sees the seeker's armed chip as a status line.** "Seeker is weighing a Radar question", no shape. The Whitehall "overhearing" moment. In solo the bot arms 3 real seconds before asking (its action floor). *Changes:* hider's Locked-and-watching screen; the seeker's arm state becomes a public event. *Risk:* in M3 arming is free, so decoys are a mind game; the hider gets 3 seconds' head start on a Veto decision, which is small against the 10-second window.

**8. The hider previews the seeker's four static shots at the spot.** NMPZ evidence symmetry: judge the spot on what the seeker could buy. *Changes:* the Finding-a-spot screen's lock row. *Risk:* four Static loads per preview; cap to the spot being considered, and the daily Static quota (8.6) must account for it.

**9. The share tells the story; the result compares the friend.** Second share line "97 → 25 → 7 → 3 · wrong station once · seen at 40 m", a struck tile for a miss, and "Friend 41 min · You 52 min" on the result card from the `t` already in the link, with an optional nickname. *Changes:* the decided four-line share, the result card, the arrival band. *Risk:* share length; a wrong-station count hints at the day's hardness to someone who hasn't played (mild; the hub is already named).

**10. A `first_round` flag, then Normal-station Easy-play.** First the metric: log first rounds separately and read found-rate and questions-to-pin. If it's poor, a browser's first daily keeps the shared station and deck but the bot plays no curses and no kill-shot veto, and a missed first daily defaults the next practice to Easy. *Changes:* round logs, bot policy per session, the practice picker default. *Risk:* the friend's `t` stops being like-for-like on a first round; it may be right to apply only to practice and leave the daily uniform.

**Rejected on the evidence, for the record:** a global leaderboard (needs server authority the exploit note already says is absent, and the retention evidence is weak); an Easy-band daily (breaks the shared station; wait for idea 10's data); photos via the Embed API (lets the seeker walk around the hider's pano); a paid speed toggle (Worldboard's answer to rounds that are too long); any rubber-banding in either bot; and telling the seeker "Right station" on surfacing (contradicts the 2026-10-05 decision that finding the station is recorded, not revealed).
