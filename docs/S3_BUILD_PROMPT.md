# Prompt: build "You hide, a bot seeks" (S3)

You're building the hider's half of Subway Seek, an online hide-and-seek game on the real NYC subway timetable with a Google Street View endgame. Today the game has one playable side: a human seeks, a bot hides. Your job is the other side, end to end: a human hides and a bot seeks, as a mode anyone can play from the homepage's "You hide" door. It's also the groundwork for the two-player match (M3): when you're done, two-player should need rooms and a swap, not new rules.

Build it faithfully to the design, in the order below. Where the design is silent, write the proposal into `DESIGN.md` and ask Charlie; don't guess at a rule.

## What exists that you build on

- **The whole round, from the seeker's side.** Hide phase (the bot hider's ride is a recorded `hiderTrail`), seeking, questions with prices, cooldowns and the 1-game-minute answer window, cards (draw on answer, hand of 6, Veto closing a question, Randomize, Discard 2 draw 3, the five curses with server enforcement), the Street View endgame (spot inside the 400 m zone, 40 m reveal, 25 m catch, on-foot questions, the 8 m search grid), the two-hour cap, the score, the replay, results in the browser, the share line.
- **A bot seeker** (`seekerBot.ts`) that plays from `SoloView` alone and can't read the hider's station, spot or hand by type. It asks the thrifty question when one is ready, waits out the answer window, rides toward the candidates re-planning at every answer, and at three or fewer stations rides to the nearest, comes up, and sweeps the street with on-foot questions priced as the sim prices them. It plays curses the way a person would. Measured on 780 rounds: Normal station in a median 38 game-min, endgame 38% of seeking, 96% found.
- **Sessions that survive a drop and a server restart**, a shared 6× clock with fast-forward and pause in practice, rate limits, signed photo URLs, the Mosaic component vocabulary, the homepage with four doors.

Not in the engine yet, and in your scope: a round driven by a human hider's intents during the hide; the hider's view and its visibility filter; a human's answer, Veto and Randomize decisions inside the answer window; Hide here, the freeze rule and the zone; the bot seeker paced by the real clock on the server; the "seeker is in your zone" alert; the hider's result.

## Rules you must keep

- **The engine stays pure**: (state, intent, time) → (new state, events). No sockets, no timers. The bot seeker's decisions are engine calls the server makes at times the engine returns (`waitUntil`).
- **One visibility filter per player.** Everything sent to the hider passes through a `hiderView`. The hider sees the seeker's live position, the trains they ride, every question as it's asked and its answer, the stations still possible as the seeker knows them, and curses landing. The hider does not see the bot's plan or beliefs. The seeker (the bot) sees only `soloView`, as today; the server must never hand the bot the round.
- **Events, not ticks.** The hider's client derives positions from the timetable and the shared clock, as the seeker's does.
- **Motion is presentation, never state.**
- **Google terms**: Street View only beside the Google map; no caching of Google's coordinates (panorama IDs only); the hider's spot must be one of Google's own panoramas (`officialImagery`). Don't recreate the Google map or panorama in a hot-reload loop while developing: use `VITE_MAP_PROVIDER=maplibre` except when the map itself is what you're checking.
- **Hidden information between the two sides** (the human is the hider now, but the same code will serve a human seeker later): the seeker's view carries nothing that depends on the hider's hand or spot except answers and card plays that affect them; every station question waits out the answer window whether or not a card is played, so the window's length leaks nothing.
- **Mosaic**: squares for game data, circles only for trains; Josefin Sans captions, Libre Franklin text; no rounded corners, shadows or emoji icons; terra cotta only for Hide here and Lock & answer; the caption budget and the interaction rules in DESIGN.md.

**Step 1. Engine: the hider's round.** In `packages/engine`:

- The answer window from the hider's side: when a question arrives, the hider has the window to decide; with no decision the true answer goes out at `answerAt`. A Veto draws nothing; the question closes for the round, as today.
- Tests for every rule above, in the style of `solo.test.ts` and `cards.test.ts`. Run `pnpm sim:match` to check nothing moved for the seeker's side.

## What not to do

- Don't build rooms, matchmaking or a second human socket. That's M3.
- Don't change the seeker's rules, prices or the bot hider. If a rule has to change for the hider's side to work, propose it in DESIGN.md first.
- Don't let the bot seeker see anything but `SoloView`. Not the round, not the hider's hand, not the spot.
- Don't send the hider's client anything about the bot's plan.
- Don't invent copy. The hider screens' wording is in DESIGN.md; where a state has none, write the line into DESIGN.md's Voice table or the screen's section and ask.
- Don't cache Google's coordinates, put Street View beside the MapLibre map, or run the Google map in a hot-reload loop.

## Done means

A person can open the homepage, choose "You hide", ride for twenty minutes of game time, come up and lock a spot, answer or veto the bot's questions in the window, play curses, watch the bot close in and walk the streets around them, and get a result card and a replay. Every rule above has a test. `pnpm test`, `pnpm typecheck`, `pnpm lint` and `pnpm sim:match` are green. The docs say what the game does.
